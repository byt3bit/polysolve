\noindent
\textbf{Polynomials and Solving Polynomial System.}
For $\alpha = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^n$ we define $\mathbf{x}^{\alpha} := x_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^{\alpha_n}$. The total degree of $\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}$ is $|\alpha|$, where $|\alpha| := \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i$. For a polynomial $f \in \Z_q[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ of total degree $d$, we adopt the following degree decomposition:
\[
f = f^{(d)} + f^{(d-1)} + \cdots + f^{(1)} + f^{(0)},
\]
where $f^{(k)}$ denotes the sum of all monomials in $f$ of total degree exactly $k$.
For a fixed monomial order $\prec$ on $\Z_q[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, \LM$(f)$ and \LT$(f)$ denote respectively the leading monomial and leading term of $f$ with respect to $\prec$. The notion of $S$-polynomial is well-known in the context of Gr\"obner bases, especially when coefficients of polynomials lie in a field. When coefficients are in a ring, the $S$-polynomial computation must be adapted. Initiated by Bruno Buchberger, the study of Gr{\"o}bner basis on rings has gained more attention~\cite{}. Indeed, Gr{\"o}bner basis theory has been extended to the so-called \emph{reduction rings}~\cite{}. 

It is well-known that \emph{Euclidean rings}, e.g. \(\Z, \Z_q, \F_q[x]\), are always reduction rings. In this context, for $f, g \in \Z_q[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, the $S$-polynomial is defined as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spoly-expanded}
    S_{fg} := \frac{\mathbf{c}}{\lc(f)}\frac{\mathbf{x}^\gamma}{\mathrm{LM}(f)} \cdot f
              - \frac{\mathbf{c}}{\lc(g)}\frac{\mathbf{x}^\gamma}{\mathrm{LM}(g)} \cdot g,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}^\gamma  = \texttt{lcm}(\mathrm{LM}(f), \mathrm{LM}(g))$ and $\mathbf{c} = \texttt{lcm}(\lc(f), \lc(g))$. 

The main issue in Gr\"obner basis computations over rings arises during the $S$-polynomial reduction, e.g. multivariate polynomial division, where the leading term of the divisor may not divide the leading term of the dividend. In this case, the reduction process is not straightforward and may require additional steps to ensure that the resulting polynomial is reduced with respect to the given set of polynomials. This can lead to increased computational complexity and may require more sophisticated algorithms to handle these cases effectively. 
In our algorithm we will devise a strategy to avoid multivariate polynomial division and thus the issues related to it. More specifically, the divisions will be replaced by simple linear algebra operations which can be efficiently implemented on rings too.

The Gr\"obner basis method is a well-known technique for solving a system of polynomials. For a complete introduction to the topic of Gr\"obner basis and its applications on reduction rings, we refer the reader to~\cite{Cox2015Ideals}. % add citation reduction ring
