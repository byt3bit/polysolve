
\subsubsection{The LWE Polynomial System.}
Each sample $(\vars{a}_i, b_i) = (\vars{a}_i, \langle\vars{a}_i, \vars{s}\rangle + e_i) \in \Z_q^n \times \Z_q$, for $1 \leq i \leq m$, allows to generate a non-linear equation of degree $d$ in the $n$ components of the secret $\vars{s}$. %which holds with probability $1 - e^{\mathcal{O}({-t^2})}$.
Considering that a common choice for the error distribution is the discrete Gaussian with mean $0$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, denoted as $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma)$,
we have
\[
\mathbb{P}\big[ e \overset{\scriptscriptstyle\$}{\gets} \chi : |e| > t \cdot \sigma \big] \leq \frac{2}{t\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-t^2/2}\in e^{\mathcal{O}({-t^2})}, \quad \textrm{for all } t>0.
\]
A sample from a Gaussian distribution take values on the interval $[-t\cdot\sigma,\dots,t\cdot\sigma]$ of $\Z_q$ with probability $1 - e^{\mathcal{O}({-t^2})}$ when representing elements in $\Z_q$ as integers in $[-\lfloor q/2 \rfloor, \dots, \lfloor q/2 \rfloor]$.
When $e \overset{\scriptscriptstyle \$}{\gets} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma)$ then $f(e)=0$ for $f(x) = x \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{t\cdot\sigma} (x+i)(x-i)$ with probability at least $1 - e^{\mathcal{O}({-t^2})}$.
The degree of $f$ is $d := 2 \cdot t \sigma + 1 < q$. %We denote by $n$ the dimension of the secret. %and by $m$ the number of samples. %and $d := 2 \cdot t + 1$.
The resulting polynomial system $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{LWE}}$ is solved using the linearization method, namely by replacing monomials with a new linearized variable and then solving the resulting linear system of equations. %The \belwe\ where $\vars{e} \in \{0,1\}^m$ was introduced in~\cite{MicPei13}. 
The \belwe\, in which $\vars{e} \in \{0,1\}^m$, is a particular instance and was introduced in~\cite{MicPei13}.

\subsubsection{Reduction of \rlwe\ to the LWE Polynomial System.}
The reduction of \rlwe\ to the LWE polynomial system is straightforward and is also given in~\cite{EC:LyuPeiReg10}. Given a \rlwe\ instance, we can represent the secret and the samples as vectors of coefficients with respect to a fixed basis of the underlying ring. This allows us to express the \rlwe\ problem as a system of polynomial equations in the coefficients of the secret.

For example, consider a sample \(a, s, e \in \Z_q[x]/(x^N + 1)\), we can write them in terms of their coefficients as follows:
\[a = a_0 + a_1 x + \dots + a_{N-1} x^{N-1},\]
\[s = s_0 + s_1 x + \dots + s_{N-1} x^{N-1},\]
\[e = e_0 + e_1 x + \dots + e_{N-1} x^{N-1}.\]
The \rlwe\ equation \(b = a \cdot s + e\) can then be expanded and rearranged to yield a system of polynomial equations in the coefficients \(s_0, s_1, \dots, s_{N-1}\) of the secret \(s\) which will denote the target unknowns. How to get those relations between the coefficients?

Let's consider the product \(a \cdot s\) in the cyclotomic ring \(\Z_q[x]/(x^N + 1)\):
\[a \cdot s = (a_0 + a_1 x + \dots + a_{N-1} x^{N-1})(s_0 + s_1 x + \dots + s_{N-1} x^{N-1}).\]
Expanding this product and reducing by the relation \(x^N = -1\) (since we are working in the cyclotomic ring), we can express the coefficients of the result as a linear combination of \(s\). Specifically, the coefficient of \(x^k\) in the product can be computed as:
\[\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_i s_{(k-i) \mod N}.\]
This gives us a system of polynomial equations in the coefficients of the secret \(s\) and the known coefficients of \(a\) and \(b\): for \(0 \le k < N\), 
\[b_k - \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_i s_{(k-i) \mod{N}} = e_k.\]

The coefficients of the error term \(e\) are small and sampled from a discrete Gaussian distribution. Therefore, assume the error coefficients \(e_k\) lie in \([-t,t]\), we can construct a polynomial \(f(x) = x \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{t} (x+i)(x-i)\) that vanishes on all possible values of the error coefficients. This means that \(f(e_k) = 0\) for all \(k\), that is \[f\left(b_k - \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_i s_{(k-i) \mod N}\right) = 0.\]

For one sample, the resulting polynomial system has \(N\) equations of degree \(d := 2t+1\) in \(N\) variables (the coefficients of the secret).

\subsubsection{Reduction of \mlwe\ to the LWE Polynomial System.}
The reduction of \mlwe\ to the LWE polynomial system is similar to the reduction of \rlwe. Again, we express the \mlwe\ problem as a system of polynomial equations in the coefficients of the secret.

Let \(\vars{a}, \vars{s} \in R_q^k\) and \(e, b \in R_q\) be the elements of one \mlwe\ sample. The \mlwe\ relation is given by:
\[b = \langle \vars{a}, \vars{s} \rangle + e = \sum_{j=1}^k a_j s_j + e.\]
For \(1 \le j \le k\), \(a_j\) and \(s_j\) can be expressed in terms of their coefficients:
\[a_j = a_{j,0} + a_{j,1}x + \dots + a_{j,N-1}x^{N-1},\]
\[s_j = s_{j,0} + s_{j,1}x + \dots + s_{j,N-1}x^{N-1}.\]

From the \rlwe\ reduction, we know how to express the product \(a_j s_j\) as a linear combination of the secret coefficients of \(s_j\). After the sum \(\sum_{j=1}^k a_j s_j\) is computed, the coefficients of the resulting polynomial can be expressed as linear combinations of the coefficients of all the secret polynomials \(s_1, \dots, s_k\) and the known coefficients of \(a_1, \dots, a_k\). 
More specifically, the coefficient of \(x^k\) in the final sum can be computed as:
\[\sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_{j,i} s_{j,(k-i) \mod N}.\]

For \(0 \le k < N\), the \mlwe\ relation can be rewritten as: $b_k - \sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_{j,i} s_{j,(k-i) \mod N} = e_k.$


The coefficients of the error polynomial \(e\) are small and sampled from a discrete Gaussian distribution. Therefore, assume the error coefficients \(e_k\) lie in \([-t,t]\), we can construct a polynomial \(f(x) = x \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{t} (x+i)(x-i)\) that vanishes on all possible values of the error coefficients. This means that \(f(e_k) = 0\) for all \(k\), that is \[f\left(b_k - \sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_{j,i} s_{j,(k-i) \mod N}\right) = 0.\]

Consequently, for one sample of \mlwe, the resulting polynomial system has \(N\) equations of degree \(d := 2t+1\) in \(Nk\) variables (the coefficients of the secret polynomials).
